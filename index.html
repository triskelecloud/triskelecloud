    Kingdom RTS

    Gather. Build. Defend. Conquer.

    🎓 First game (guided)

    New here? Start with five short steps: train villagers, build a house, a farm and a barracks, then train soldiers. The enemy is Easy and will not attack for a while.

    ⚔ Skirmish

    The classic game: grow your village, advance through the ages and destroy the enemy Town Center before it destroys yours.

    📜 Campaign

    Six story missions: settle, hold the walls, raid, rule the lake, storm a castle and win the crown.

    🌊 Endless

    Build an economy, then survive endless, escalating waves from every edge of the map. Boss waves every 5th wave.

    🛠 Sandbox

    Build any city, paint the map and stage battles between up to six colours of armies. Unlimited resources.

    Works offline. Save from the pause menu (Esc). The game also autosaves every minute.

    Campaign

    Endless waves

    You start with a Town Center in the middle of the map and 3 minutes to prepare. Waves come from more edges as they grow; every 5th wave is a boss wave with knights and catapults. Between waves you get a short build phase — call the next wave early for bonus points.

    High scores

    Custom skirmish

    Up to 6 kingdoms, free-for-all or in teams. Allies share vision and never fight each other; the last side with a Town Center wins. The map grows with the number of players and the population cap per player shrinks a little so big games stay smooth.

    Nations & heroes, 500–1300 AD

    Every nation has a perk, a unique unit, two unique technologies (at the Blacksmith) and two or three heroes trained at the Town Center (one hero at a time). Your team colour still marks your units; the flag shows your nation.

      Game modes

      Skirmish: you vs a full AI kingdom, or a Custom game with 2–6 kingdoms (colours, free-for-all or Teams 1–3, AI level per player). Campaign: six story missions with objectives (top-left panel; Pause → Objectives to re-read the briefing), each unlocking the next. Endless: defend your Town Center against escalating waves from the map edges, with a boss every 5th wave; build between waves or press Next wave now (N) for bonus points. Sandbox: build a city and stage battles.

      Sandbox editor

      Open/close with Tab or the 🛠 button. Pick an owner (any of the game's 2–6 players), then a unit, building or terrain from the palette and click/drag on the map (Shift+click places 5 units). Erase removes anything under the brush. Right-click or Esc drops the tool so you can select and command Blue as usual. Nothing fights while Staging; press ▶ Start battle and the other armies charge (yours too if the charge box is ticked). Press it again to freeze the fight and keep editing. Options: unlimited resources, instant build/train, fog of war and the computer players' AI (off / passive / active - each needs its own Town Center). Save named layouts and reload them from the editor or the Sandbox menu. The palette includes riderless Horses, Scouts, Guerrilla Archers, Knights, wild animals (deer, boar, rabbits, pheasants, wolves, bears, lions, tigers, wild horses) and tall grass terrain. Villagers can hunt even while Staging; wolves only attack once a battle starts. The usual unit limit applies (200 per team, 720 on the map; Large: 250 / 960): past it you'll see "Unit limit reached".

      Undo / redo: Ctrl+Z (Cmd+Z on Mac) undoes, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes, or use the ↶ Undo / ↷ Redo buttons. Every placement, erase and terrain stroke can be undone; one click or drag stroke = one step (up to 200 steps). History is kept only while Staging: starting or stopping a battle clears it, and loading a layout starts a fresh history.

      Goal (Skirmish)

      Destroy the enemy Town Center (red, far corner of the map) – with more players, every hostile Town Center; a player with none left is eliminated. You lose if your own Town Center falls. Allies share vision and never fight you. With more players the map grows and each player's unit limit shrinks a little (2p 200, 3p 170, 4p 150, 5p 130, 6p 115; the whole map holds at most 720 units) so battles stay smooth. The Custom game's Large army limit raises that to 250–150 per player and 960 on the map, and Huge (computers only) to 320–180 per player and 1440 on the map: bigger battles, but slower PCs may stutter. Barbarian treasure camps (8 at the start) are scattered around the map: clear the guards and destroy the treasure chest to loot it. Bigger camps hold bigger hoards, and some camps are hidden in forest or tall grass. A looted camp reappears somewhere new after a few minutes, so treasure never runs out. Pause shows the scoreboard, the Graphics setting (Ultra: High plus extras; High: detailed units and buildings, waving flags, chimney smoke, varied trees, water caustics, particles, soft fog; Low: fastest) and Big battle mode (Auto: the game switches to simple unit dots on its own when a huge fight gets slow). The camera is Flat top-down. The enemy gathers, builds, researches and advances ages just like you, and attacks when its army is ready (estimate in the top bar). Killing its villagers and boats slows it down.

      Economy

      • Select villagers and right-click trees (wood), grey rocks (stone), yellow rocks (gold), berry bushes or shore fish (food).
      • Villagers carry 10 at a time to the Town Center or a Storehouse. Farms give endless food; Houses add +5 population.
      • Dock (C): build on water touching the shore. Train Fishing Boats and right-click fish shoals in open water; boats unload at the Dock. Canoe (v1.8.7): train a small Canoe at the Dock (80 wood). It carries up to 8 foot warriors (swordsmen, spearmen, archers, guerrillas, scouts) across water. Bring warriors to the shore, right-click the Canoe to board, sail it to the enemy shore, then right-click a shore tile (or press Unload) to land them for an attack. Warriors aboard cannot fight, and if the Canoe sinks they are lost with it. The computer players do not use Canoes yet.
      • Market (v1.8.5): build it in the Feudal Age (150 wood, 50 stone) from the villager build menu, then click it to trade in 100s. Buy 100 food or wood for 80 gold, or 100 stone for 100 gold; sell 100 food or wood for 50 gold, or 100 stone for 70 gold. Buying then selling the same resource loses gold, so trade only when you are stuck on one resource. The computer players do not use the Market yet.
      • Diplomacy (v1.8.6, AI only): press the Diplomacy button in the top bar (or Pause → Diplomacy) in a Skirmish or Custom game. Send 100 of any resource as tribute for 30 trust, then offer an alliance at 30 trust. An allied computer player will not attack you and will fight your other enemies instead. Trade with them at Market rates, from their own stock. Keep trust at 60 or they may walk away after 10 minutes; break the alliance any time from the same panel. Teaming up with friends on a server comes later — this is computer players only.
      • Hunting: right-click a deer, boar, rabbit or pheasant with villagers. They spear it from a few tiles away, then butcher the carcass (faster than berries) and carry the meat to a Town Center, Storehouse or the new Mill (N, food only). Carcasses slowly rot, and when one is used up the hunters move on to the next animal nearby. Deer are quick and bolt (the whole herd runs) but give lots of food; boar give even more but fight back – lure one with three or four villagers or shoot it with archers. Archers, scouts and other soldiers can kill animals too (right-click them). Sheep (v1.8.2): right-click a sheep with a villager and it follows that villager instead of running away. Walk it home to your Town Center – once it gets there it stays put – then right-click it again with a villager to butcher it for 100 food. New wild animals (deer, sheep, rabbits, pheasants, boar) also wander in over time, so hunting never runs out forever. Wild horses (v1.8.3): small herds keep appearing on open grass away from towns, and can be tamed and ridden just like the first herds. Fish (v1.8.3): fish keep coming back to water near existing fish and shores, a few tiles at a time, so a Dock's fishing grounds refill after they are fished out instead of becoming a waste.
      • Wolves roam the wilds in small packs and go after lone units – walk in groups or escort your scouts. They give no food. Turn them off in the Custom game / Sandbox setup.
      • Predators (v1.2): bears in the woods, lions on open, dry ground and tigers in dense forest and tall grass. They hit hard, pick off lone villagers and scouts and go straight for loose horses – keep yours in a Horse Pen or by a Stable. Badly hurt, they run. Killed, they leave meat (a bear a lot, the big cats less). Toggle Predators in the Custom game / Sandbox setup.
      • Wild horses graze between the kingdoms: walk any land unit up to one (right-click it) and it joins you as a riderless Horse, ready to ride.
      • Repair: right-click a damaged building with villagers. Costs a fraction of its build cost, paid as it heals.

      Ages & technology

      • Advance to the Feudal Age and then the Castle Age at the Town Center.
      • Feudal: Archery Range, Watch Tower, Blacksmith, Stable (Horses), War Galley, plus economy and armour upgrades.
      • Castle: Knights at the Stable, Siege Workshop (Catapults with splash damage), Guard Tower, Fortified Walls.
      • Town Center: ages plus Loom (L), Town Bell (B), Hunting Lodge (H), Wheelbarrow/Hand Cart (C), Town Watch/Patrol (T), Masonry (K), Fortified TC (F), Crop Rotation (R), Coinage/Banking (G). After Town Bell, select the TC and press B to Ring Bell (nearby villagers hide) and B again for All Clear.
      • Storehouse: Double-Bit Axe, Gold Mining, Stone Mining, Horse Collar, Bow Saw. Blacksmith: attack and armour. Dock: Gillnets. Press F2 or Techs for the full tech tree with costs and times.

      Military

      • Scout (Town Center, V: cheap, quick, sees far, the weakest fighter), Swordsman (vs. archers and buildings), Spearman (vs. swords and all cavalry), Archer (vs. spears), Guerrilla Archer (Archery Range, W, Feudal: a fast, unarmored scout archer with a light bow), Knight (a swordsman on an armoured horse; vs. archers and siege), Catapult (vs. buildings and groups), War Galley (vs. boats and the shore).
      • Horses: the Stable trains riderless horses. Villagers, swordsmen, spearmen, archers, guerrillas and scouts ride one by right-clicking it or with J (Mount): they keep their weapon, move faster and get the horse's health as a second bar. O dismounts. Mounted villagers can't work: they hop off at the job and leave the horse beside it.
      • Horse and rider take damage separately (the horse takes about 30% of sword hits, 60% of arrows and 70% of spear thrusts; the rider the rest). If the horse dies the rider falls, stunned but alive; if the rider dies the horse stays, and anyone can steal it: right-click an enemy's riderless horse with a unit that can ride. The AI steals horses too.
      • Cavalry charge: 9 or more riders who gallop 3+ tiles together hit with a heavy first blow, knocking infantry back ("CHARGE!"). Only spearmen stop it - the charge breaks on their spears and hurts the horses, even more if they stand still to brace.
      • Armor choice: select one unit or a group and press V (Armor) or E (Unarmor). Armor costs gold (heavier armor costs more) and needs your Town Center, Barracks, Blacksmith or Stable within 7 tiles; taking it off is free but not refunded. Unarmored units are faster but take 15% more damage and lose their flat armor. Q puts barding on (or takes it off) the selected horses, ridden or riderless.
      • Guerrilla Archers are camouflaged next to trees (any tile touching a forest): the enemy can't see or target them until they shoot (they stay revealed about 4 s after their last shot) or an enemy comes within about 1.5 tiles. On horseback they lose the camouflage. They are the second-fastest foot unit (after scouts) but slower than any horse, and take 15% extra damage. Yours shimmer while hidden. The AI trains them and sets ambushes at forest edges.
      • Spy (v1.8.9): train at the Stone Keep in the Castle Age, 1000 food, max 5 at a time. Fragile (30 hp, no armor, like a Guerrilla Archer) and as fast as a Scout, with a dark cloak and dagger. He hides next to trees, buildings and walls (any tile touching one) until he attacks (revealed about 4 s) or an enemy comes within about 1.5 tiles; enemy buildings do not spot him while he is using building cover. His first strike while ready kills a normal unit outright (not heroes, elephants or buildings — those take normal sword damage), then he does normal sword damage for 60 s until the instant kill reloads. He cannot ride a horse, and the computer players do not train Spies yet.
      • Gold Panning Hut (v1.8.10): build it on the shore, next to water (Feudal Age, 120 wood + 50 stone, click-only in the build menu). It pans gold from the water by itself, 0.20 gold/s, no villager needed — slow, so it tops up a long game instead of replacing gold mines. Click it to upgrade: Sluice Box (300 gold + 100 wood) to 0.45 gold/s, then Water Wheel (600 gold + 200 wood + 100 stone) to 0.80 gold/s. Those upgrades are expensive on purpose — the first pays back in about 20 minutes of gold, the second in about 29 minutes — so it is a long-term benefit. It works by any water (lakes, rivers, sea); there is no separate stream terrain yet. It can be attacked and destroyed like any building, and then the gold stops. The computer players do not build it yet.
      • Armor weight: every unit is Unarmored (villager, guerrilla: +15% damage taken), Light (scout, archer), Medium (spearman: −10%) or Heavy (swordsman, knight: −20%). Heavier armor is slower on foot and slows a horse a little; barding (the knight's warhorse) slows it more but the horse takes 10% less damage. Horses are much faster than before; a riderless horse is the fastest unit. The unit panel shows armor and speed. Group moves march at the slowest unit's pace.
      • Tall grass hides your units from the enemy unless an enemy comes within about 2 tiles (4 for riders) or they attack. Movement through it is slower. Your hidden units look faded.
      • Unit limit: 125 units per player and 320 on the map, so big battles stay smooth.
      • Wall archers (v0.10): right-click your own wall with archers, crossbowmen, longbowmen or guerrillas selected: they climb onto the wall walk, 2 per segment (extras spread along the connected wall). Up there they shoot with +1 range, melee below can't reach them, and enemy arrows only sometimes get past the parapet (reduced damage). If the segment is destroyed they fall off and take damage.
      • Horse pens (v0.10): build a Horse Pen (P, 40 wood, walkable 3x3 paddock). Riderless horses inside a pen, or right next to a Stable, stay put. Loose horses get a ! marker, wander off after ~25 s and run wild after ~2.5 min (a wild horse anyone can tame). Stable-trained horses spawn straight into the nearest pen; the AI builds one and keeps its horses in it.
      • Siege ladders (v0.10): research Siege Ladders at the Barracks (L, 75 wood). Foot soldiers then right-click an enemy wall to set a ladder and climb (4 s, taking extra damage). Archers manning that wall or the next segment can push the ladder down (fall damage). At the top they fight the wall archers, then drop down inside. The AI uses ladders too, so man your walls.
      • Formations (v0.10): with 2+ soldiers selected use the Line / Box / Wedge / Skirmish / Column / Schiltron buttons or Alt+1-6. Group moves keep the shape at the slowest unit's pace, melee in front and ranged behind. Box: -15% melee from flank/rear. Wedge: +15% on the first hit. Skirmish: -15% from arrows. Schiltron (spearmen): +20% vs cavalry, -20% from cavalry.
      • Shield Wall formation (v1.8.8, general, not hero-only): select foot swordsmen, spearmen, archers or guerrillas of the English, Vikings, Franks, Byzantines, Crusaders, Scots (Scots as a schiltron-like shield/pike wall) or Japanese (added at your request) and press Shield Wall in the command panel, press it again to leave. You do not need a hero, and it still works after your hero falls. Front is where the unit faces. Arrows from the front do 75% less damage, cavalry hitting the front do 40% less, arrows and blows from the side or back do normal damage, and you move 35% slower in formation. Archers in the wall still shoot normally. If enemies hit you from a second direction 90 degrees or more away within 2 seconds, you are surrounded: that hit does 25% extra damage and the wall breaks at once. A pale shield arc in front of the unit shows it is in formation. This is separate from the hero ability also called Shield Wall, which is unchanged (+3/+3 armour for 10 s) and can stack on top. The computer players do not use the formation yet.
      • Mixed selection and groups (v0.10): the selection panel lists each unit type with a count; click one to keep only that type, or shift-click to drop it. Ctrl+click or double-click a unit selects all of that type on screen. Ctrl+1-9 sets a control group and 1-9 recalls it (tap twice to jump the camera). , selects the whole army and / selects idle soldiers.
      • Garrison: right-click your own building with archers selected (villagers too, into a Town Center, Tower or House). Inside they are safe and heal slowly; archers shoot from it with +1 range (one arrow each, max 5 extra per volley). Capacity: Town Center 10, Tower 5, Gate 5, House 2, others 3-4. A shield badge shows the count; select the building and press U (Ungarrison) to send them out. If the building is destroyed everyone jumps out. The enemy AI hides its villagers in its Town Center when raided.
      • Group moves arrive in formation: a line for up to 8 units, otherwise a box, melee in front, archers behind, siege at the back.
      • Nations (v0.9, 500–1300 AD): pick one of 20 historic nations (v1.0 added the Seljuk Turks, Sasanian Persians, Japanese and Chinese) on the main menu, the Campaign/Endless/Sandbox screens or per player in a Custom game (or Random; computer players pick too). Each has an always-on perk, a unique unit, two unique techs (at the Blacksmith) and its period flag on your buildings and the scoreboard. ⚑ Nations & heroes on the main menu shows every card.
      • v1.5: Sharper supersampled unit/animal sprites, more walk/attack frames, regional windows & doors, dock styling, bigger lion mane, ground pebbles/paths/cliffs, polished UI icons — all pre-baked (no frame-rate cost).
      • v1.4: Deselect button (phones) + Esc; crowd/flank/rear melee bonuses; shields block ~20% of arrows from the front/sides; five stances (Aggressive, Defensive, Stand Ground, Hold Fire, Guard) with Alt hotkeys; Funnel (Alt+7) and Surround (Alt+8) formations.
      • v1.3: a visual pass at no frame cost (High): smooth, blended ground with soft patches and forest floors, water that shades from shallow turquoise to deep blue, buildings with a cast shadow, contact shadow and sunlit edges (painted at double resolution, then downsampled), and regional fortifications – palisades for Norse and Steppe nations, mud-brick with rounded merlons for Islamic and Indian ones, grey brick under dark tiles for Japan and China.
      • v1.2: predators — bears in the forests, lions on open dry ground, tigers in dense forest and tall grass. They stalk lone villagers, scouts and above all loose horses (keep them in a pen or by a Stable), hit hard and run when badly hurt. A dead bear gives 220 food, a lion or tiger 90. Switch them off with Predators in Custom game / Sandbox. Units and animals also get a soft baked outline and rim light.
      • v1.1: every nation now looks like itself — regional building styles (Western stone & timber, Norse/Celtic turf & carved longhouses, Mediterranean red tile & domes, Islamic domes, minarets & tilework, Mongol gers, Indian shikhara temples, Japanese and Chinese curved roofs & pagodas) and regional soldier gear (helmets, shields and armor from kite shields and nasal helms to kabuto, turbans and lamellar). Team colour and the banner stay on every building.
      • v1.0: 4 new nations — Seljuk Turks (Ghulam cavalry; Tughril Beg, Alp Arslan), Persians (Sasanian Savaran; Khosrow I, Bahram Chobin), Japanese (Samurai; Minamoto no Yoshitsune, Taira no Kiyomori), Chinese (Repeating Crossbow; Emperor Taizong of Tang, Yue Fei). The computer now plans its Castle Age and fields Castle Age unique units (War Elephants, Templars, Cataphracts, Teutonic Knights...), and sends its archers up onto its own walls when you attack.
      • Heroes: train one at the Town Center (Y/J/O, Feudal Age, 200 food 150 gold). One hero at a time. Heroes are very tough, give nearby soldiers an aura (attack, armor, speed or fire rate) and have an ability (A: War Cry, Lead the Charge, Shield Wall, Volley, Rally the Wounded or Terror) with a cooldown. When your hero falls you can train another.
      • Walls block paths (drag a line). Gates (K) open for your units and stay shut to enemies. Place one on a wall, or select a wall and press K.

      Mouse

      • Left-click or drag a box to select. Shift adds to the selection. Double-click selects all of that type on screen.
      • Right-click: move / gather / attack / build / repair / set rally point. Shift+right-click queues orders.
      • Scroll wheel zooms smoothly toward the cursor. Middle-drag (fling to glide) or the screen edges pan. Click the minimap to jump.

      Keyboard

      • WASD/Arrows pan · Space Town Center · . idle villager · , army
      • Build (villagers): H House F Farm G Storehouse B Barracks C Dock L Wall K Gate R Archery T Tower I Blacksmith Y Stable Z Siege Workshop P Horse Pen N Mill
      • Unit commands: X Stop, A hero ability, J Mount, O Dismount, U Ungarrison. v1.0 hotkey audit: Horse Pen moved O→P (clashed with Dismount for mounted villagers) and hero ability Z→A (Z clashed with Siege Workshop when a hero is selected with villagers; V is Armor). L is Loom at the Town Center and Siege Ladders at the Barracks — different panels, no clash.
      • Train: Q/E, V Scout, Archery Range W Guerrilla, Stable Q Horse / E Knight (shift-click for x5) · J mount · O dismount · V armor / E unarmor the selection · Q barding on/off. Research: the letter shown on each tech button (e.g. E on the Town Center for the next age).
      • K convert the selected wall to a gate · X stop · U ungarrison · Del demolish/cancel
      • Esc/P pause (volume settings) · M mute · Ctrl+S save · +/- speed

      Touch

      • Tap to select. With units selected, tap the map to command them.
      • Drag to pan (flick to glide), pinch to zoom, long-press then drag to box-select.
      • To build, tap a spot to preview it, then tap it again to confirm.
